Handling Lyophilized Research Peptides

8 min read · Storage & Handling

Lyophilized (freeze-dried) research peptides are dissolved in the lab so their identity and quality can be measured. This page covers general handling principles only. It does not give volumes, ratios or concentrations, because those depend on the specific assay and are set by the laboratory's own methods.

Choosing a solvent class

The right solvent depends on the peptide's sequence and the assay. Laboratories typically consider aqueous solvents for hydrophilic sequences, dilute acids for sequences that are poorly soluble in neutral water, and organic solvents such as DMSO for hydrophobic sequences. Always check the compound's solubility notes first, and confirm with a small test portion.

Good handling habits

  • Let a dry vial reach room temperature before opening it, so condensation doesn't reach the solid.
  • Mix gently. Vigorous shaking can foam or stress some sequences.
  • Use low-binding tubes when surface loss could affect a measurement.
  • Use clean laboratory technique throughout.

What to record

Peptide name, lot number, solvent, date and storage conditions. A complete record ties every measurement back to the lot it came from.

Holding dissolved material

Dissolved peptides are less stable than the dry solid. Keep short-term samples refrigerated at 36–46°F, freeze longer-term aliquots at or below −4°F, and limit freeze-thaw cycles. See the storage guide for more detail.

Research use only. Not for human or veterinary use.
